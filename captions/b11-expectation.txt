Lesson 1.1 of The Math of Money: from coin flips to stochastic calculus and Black–Scholes, one reel at a time.

Today: random variables and expected value, the idea behind every price on Wall Street.

The math, step by step:
• A random variable X is a number decided by chance (like a die roll)
• Expected value = a weighted average: E[X] = Σ x · P(X = x)
• Fair die: (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6) / 6 = 3.5, the balance point of the distribution
• It's the long-run average: 10,000 simulated rolls settle on 3.5
• A bet that wins $10 with p = 0.3 and loses $5 with p = 0.7 has E = 3 − 3.5 = −$0.50, and 10,000 simulated bets agree
• Linearity: E[X + Y] = E[X] + E[Y], even when X and Y are dependent, so two dice average 7

Where it's used: a casino's edge (one number on American roulette: E = −2/38 ≈ −5.3¢ per $1) and option pricing (price = discounted expected payoff), which we'll derive later in the series.
