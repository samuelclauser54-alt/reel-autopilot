Nobody can predict a stock's next move, but an option on it has an exact price. Getting there takes stochastic calculus.

The setup (all simulated, real math):
• Call option: pay max(price − $100, 0) in one year. Stock at $100, volatility 20%, interest 5%
• Black–Scholes price: $10.45
• The surprise: raise the stock's expected growth from 5% to 20% and the average payoff jumps to $23.97, but the price stays $10.45

Why: own the option and short the right amount of stock (delta hedging). In this simulation the unhedged P&L swings up to $8.98, and the hedged one stays within $0.69. No risk means you earn the bank rate, so the expected growth drops out.

To do the hedge exactly, you need calculus on paths with no slope anywhere. The key rule is (dW)² = dt: over 1,000 random steps the sizes add up to 25.2, but the squares add up to 0.988 ≈ t.
