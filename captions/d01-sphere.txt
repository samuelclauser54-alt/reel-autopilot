Put a ball snugly inside a can. The ball fills exactly 2/3 of it. Archimedes was so proud of this that he wanted a sphere and cylinder on his tomb.

His argument, step by step:
1. Take half: a dome of radius r inside a can of height r
2. Slice at height h. Pythagoras: the dome's slice has area πr² − πh²
3. πr² is the can's slice. πh² is the slice of an upside-down cone (radius h at height h)
4. So at every height, dome slice = can slice − cone slice (a disk with the same area as a ring)
5. Stack the slices: dome = πr³ − ⅓πr³ = ⅔πr³, so ball = ⁴⁄₃πr³
6. Can around the ball = 2πr³ → ratio exactly 2/3

Bonus: dV/dr = 4πr² is the surface area, because growing r adds a thin skin.
