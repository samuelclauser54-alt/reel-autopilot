Every AI you've ever used is secretly doing one thing: bending space. 🌀

What a neural network actually does, step by step (Math of AI, Ep. 1):

1️⃣ The problem: separate blue points from red. They're tangled, so no straight line can do it (the best one still gets 22 points wrong).
2️⃣ A matrix moves every point of the plane at once: x → Wx + b. Lines stay straight, and every area gets multiplied by |det W|. That's linear algebra.
3️⃣ Then a nonlinear "bend": tanh squashes every coordinate into (−1, 1). Without this step, stacking matrices would just give you one more matrix.
4️⃣ Matrix, bend, matrix, bend… and the tangled data becomes splittable by ONE straight line. Run it backwards and that line turns out to be a curve snaking between the two groups.
5️⃣ Who picks the 15 numbers? Gradient descent (my earlier video). Watch accuracy sit at 90% for over a thousand steps, then snap to 100%.

This is real. I trained this exact network myself (2 → 2 → 2, tanh). Every frame shows its real weights, and the training replay uses its real checkpoints.
GPT-3 runs the same matrix-then-nonlinearity recipe (plus attention, which mixes words together) in 12,288 dimensions across 96 layers, with 175 billion numbers.
