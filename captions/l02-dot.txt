Why does "multiply matching numbers and add them up" measure how well two things match? This is the dot product, the operation inside every neural network.

The picture:
1. A 3×3 template: blue weights (+1) want ink, red weights (−1) don't
2. Multiply each pixel by its weight: ink on blue → positive, ink on red → negative, no ink → 0
3. Add up: vertical bar = 1.5, the same bar sideways = −0.5
4. Twice as dark → exactly double (3.0). The dot product cares about strength, not just shape

The geometry: a·b = |a||b| cos θ. Same direction → big and positive, 90° → 0, opposite → negative.

The 2-line proof: |a − b|² = |a|² + |b|² − 2a·b (algebra) and also = |a|² + |b|² − 2|a||b|cos θ (law of cosines). Everything else cancels.

Divide by both lengths and you get cosine similarity, which embedding search uses to decide that two sentences mean the same thing.
